Twenty years ago this month, in my first year of college, someone I had a crush on invited me to a contra dance. We had already missed the beginner workshop, so she gave me an impromptu lesson on how to perform the contra dance swing in [Tappan Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tappan_Square). I was immediately captivated and consumed – in the best way.

I grew up feeling physically awkward and out of touch with my body. The grace I finally felt able to summon while contra dancing[^1] was intoxicating. Contra became a gateway to other forms of movement. I dabbled in swing, blues, and fusion. I did some waltz. I joined a [rapper sword team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapper_sword) for a time. I've dipped a toe into Scottish Country Dance and Scandinavian dances and who can remember what else. But contra dance – as well as, more recently, its close relative English Country Dance – has continued to feel like home.

[^1]: Though in retrospect I suspect I was less graceful in those early years than I imagined.

It's hard to overstate how important contra dance has been to me. I don't know how to love something without wanting to see every side of it, so I became an organizer. The [events](https://www.dandelionromp.com) [I've](https://youthdanceweekend.org) [organized](https://www.bidadance.org) have been some of the proudest accomplishments of my life, ephemeral as they are. I learned how to call dances. (And then, much later, I learned how to call dances *well*.) I joined the board of a [folk music and dance camp in the woods](https://pinewoods.org).

The majority of my friends in adulthood have been people I dance with and, with only one or two exceptions[^2], every romantic partner as well. I truly don't know how I would meet people and make friends without it. Social dance has given me something to pass the time with, a community to rely on, a social portal anywhere I've gone, and – often – even *purpose*.

[^2]: In a list that is of non-trivial length.

What an incredible twenty years it has been.
